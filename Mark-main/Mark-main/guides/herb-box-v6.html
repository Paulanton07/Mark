Build guide

X-Leg Herb Trough your build · v6

A clear, in-order guide to making the two X-leg frames, fitting the long walls, and scribing the end pieces to fit.

Build at a glance

  • Two X-leg frames support a trough made from 900 mm-long planks.
  • Use four legs, each 700 mm × 80 mm × 23 mm, with one end cut at 33°.
  • Use eight long wall planks, each 900 mm × 80 mm × 23 mm: four front and four back.
  • Set the top inside gap of each X to about 286 mm, measured inside corner to inside corner.
  • The legs are not notched. One leg lies full-thickness on top of the other.
  • The six end pieces are scribed from an offcut and fit inside the V of the legs; they are not all cut to a preset length.

All dimensions are in millimetres. The guide shows the 900 mm trough with four plank courses on each long side.

The top gap sets the X. Make both frames to the same gap.

1. Materials and cut list

Prepare the straight-cut parts first. Leave the end pieces until the long sides are fitted so you can scribe them to the actual V.

Materials and fixings

  • Untreated pine or hardwood. Use untreated timber for the food-growing bed.
  • 35 mm part-threaded screws for the leg crossings and side courses.
  • Optional: a 6 mm or 10 mm bolt through each crossing for a more rigid joint.
  • Use stainless or coated fixings only.
  • Apply wood oil (Woodoc) to the outside and the feet.

Two rules to keep in mind

  • Cut all four feet with one saw setting. Use the same 33° setting and stop; cutting a leg separately can leave the trough standing on three feet.
  • Lock each crossing. One screw can let the X scissor. Add 3–4 screws, or use a bolt.

2. Build it in four stages

Follow the stages in order. Fit and level the long walls before closing either end.

1

Make the two X frames

Cut and mark the legs. Use four 700 mm legs, each 80 mm wide and 23 mm thick. Cut one end of every leg at 33°, using the same saw setting. Mark the crossing point at the centre of each leg: 350 mm along and 40 mm across.

Assemble the first X. Clamp a straight plank to the work surface. Press the angled foot of the first leg flush against it. Lay the second leg on top of the first to form the X—do. Drill a pilot hole, then screw through the crossing with a 35 mm part-threaded screw. Add 3–4 screws, or use a 6 mm or 10 mm bolt, so the joint cannot scissor.

Set the gap and repeat. Set the top inside gap to about 286 mm, measured inside corner to inside corner. Make the second frame to match. The build-photo reference also shows small spacer blocks at the shoulder, filling the gap between the angled leg head and the underside of the first planks; the photographed marks are approximately 60 mm and 75 mm.

Check before moving on: All four feet sit flat, both frames have the same 286 mm inside top gap, and each crossing is locked.
Why: A single saw setting keeps the feet level. Matching the two X frames gives the trough a level support at both ends.

2

Stand the frames and fit the first long planks

Stand the two X frames parallel, one at each end of the trough. Fit one 900 mm plank along the inside of each V: one on the front and one on the back. These are the first planks of the long walls.

Cut the first plank’s inner long edge to the confirmed 22.5° angle so it sits snugly in the V. The second starter plank sits 23 mm higher; trim 23 mm from its edge so the tops of the two starter planks line up.

Keep the plank ends lined up. The back planks finish flush with the outside legs; the front planks stand 23 mm proud, matching the build photos.

Check before moving on: Both starter planks sit snugly in their Vs, their tops are level with each other, and the back / front offsets are correct.
Why: The bevel lets each plank sit against the inside of the V. Level starter planks give the remaining courses a consistent starting point.
Future photo / sketch: front and back starter planks seated in the V
3

Build up the two long sides

Add the remaining three planks above the starter plank on each side. This makes four courses on the front and four on the back—eight 900 mm wall planks altogether.

Fix the planks along the inside faces of the X-frame legs. They run between the frames; they do not sit on top of the leg ends. Keep the plank ends lined up and both long sides at the same height. Maintain the back planks flush to the outside legs and the front planks 23 mm proud.

Check before moving on: Both long sides are secure, level, and the same height. Do this before closing the ends.
Why: The two X frames support the long planks at each end. Matching the sides gives the end pieces a consistent opening to fit.
Future photo / sketch: four courses on each long side; show flush back and 23 mm proud front
4

Close the ends with scribed pieces

Work on one end at a time. Hold an offcut horizontally inside the V at the bottom. Mark the angle from the inside to make the first end piece. Cut along the mark and test-fit it inside the V.

Use a longer offcut to mark the next piece above the first. Cut it, then glue and screw it on top of the first piece. Mark and fit a third piece above the second. Repeat the process at the other end.

Use glue and screws: end grain is weak, so the glue carries the load while the screws hold the pieces as the glue sets.

Final check: All six end pieces fit inside the Vs—three at each end—and the end courses follow the actual frame rather than a guessed measurement.
Why: Scribing each piece from the fitted opening accounts for the real shape of the V and avoids relying on a preset length.

3. How the trough sits in the frame

End view is schematic. The long sides lean inward at 22.5°; the leg tops stand proud above the rim.

33° — the leg

The 33° cut is for the leg foot and its lean. Use one saw setting for all four legs. Nothing else in the build is cut at 33°.

22.5° — the V

The sides lean in at 22.5°. Cut the inner long edge of the lowest long plank to match. Scribe each end piece above it from an offcut held in the actual V.

Reference details from the build photos (14 Oct): the legs cross one on top of the other with no notch; small spacer blocks fill the shoulder gap; the back wall is flush with the outside leg and the front wall stands 23 mm proud.
